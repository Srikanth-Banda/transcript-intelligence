Scene 2 of 7 · decisions that shaped the pipeline

Decisions that shaped the pipeline

The problem

Every call needs a type, and a title rule only works where titles say “Support Case”.

Options I weighed

  • Title rule alonemisses 3 of 70 customer calls
  • Model on all callsadds error where metadata is certain
  • Email check, then model on the openingtitle withheld from the model

What I chose

Calls with no outside attendee are internal (30, no model). A model reads the opening of the other 70 and picks support or external.

Why

Withholding the title keeps the title rule a real cross-check. A model that sees “Support Case #9279” would just copy it.

Known weakness

No ground truth. The title-rule cross-check is wrong on 3 of 70, and my own review of 15 items is small.

The problem

I need categories a manager can act on. Asking a model to discover themes gave a different list each run.

Options I weighed

  • Re-cluster every batchcategories shift under the dashboard
  • Hand-written list, no discoveryimposes my assumptions
  • Model proposes once, a human freezes8 themes, each with an owner

What I chose

Freeze an 8-theme taxonomy and classify every call against it. A call that fits nothing goes to Other (0% here).

Why

Two discovery runs overlapped only 0.37. The human-level freeze is the real taxonomy, not the raw proposal.

Known weakness

Finalised by my assistant under delegation, built from the same calls it labels. Small themes have few examples.

The problem

The provided score is already an AI output. I need my own measure, with a quote behind every score.

Options I weighed

  • Use the provided scoreadds no new insight
  • Word list or ready-made classifieroutage words read negative; no quotes
  • One model pass, written 1-5 anchorscustomer and staff scored per turn

What I chose

One pass per call: a 1-5 score against written anchors, a label for every turn, customer and staff apart. The whole-call score exists for all 100 calls.

Why

In support calls the customer carries the negativity, so side matters. Internal calls have no customer, so the whole call is the unit.

Known weakness

A model judges tone, with its own bias. Anchors mix tone and outcome. It agrees with the provided labels on 80% of turns: consistent, not proof.

The problem

One model for all three stages, picked on evidence from our own calls, not a leaderboard.

Options I weighed

  • Cheapest model per stagemy own pre-written rule
  • A larger modelcostlier, no measured gain
  • Gemini 3.8 Flash for all stagesbake-off on our data

What I chose

Gemini 3.8 Flash for every stage, provider pinned, temperature 0, fixed seed. Any change means re-running the stage for all calls.

Why

It was the only model that honoured both settings and gave the same Stage 0 answers across repeat runs.

Known weakness

My pre-written rule scored format, not correctness, so it just picked the cheapest. I overrode it and I say so.

So what

Each choice is judged as a stage that runs again on every new batch of calls, and each one carries a weakness I wrote down.

How far to trust it, and what I would not claim

My hand-checks

Stage 0 · call type15/15agree with the model; 12/15 with the title rule
Stage 1 · theme20/20
Stage 1 · product area19/20
Stage 1 · outage flag17/20all 3 misses: I flagged it, the model did not
Stage 2 · call score15/15within 0.5; 9 exact; rank correlation 0.91

Caveats

  • Small samples: 15, 20 and 15 calls, my own review.
  • I cannot confirm I did not see the model's answers first.
  • Taxonomy and anchors were finalised under delegation to my assistant, from the same calls.
  • The data looks synthetic: call shapes are very uniform.
  • The outage flag is probably a floor: it missed 3 of 20 and never over-flagged, so 42 is a low count.

What I would not claim

  • That the outage caused the March dip or the competitor mentions.
  • That the follow-up list predicts churn. It ranks calls for a human.
  • That the scores are correct. They are consistent with the provided score, itself an AI output.
  • That this holds for real customers or other months.
